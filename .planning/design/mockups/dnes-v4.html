Read as an object, not a screen

hero The moon is built from three layered radial gradients (lit face, bronze near-light, violet far-light) plus a blurred depth-twin behind it and a grain overlay — a lit sphere with weight, not a two-tone icon. The dark hemisphere is a soft multiply overlay, not a hard-edged mask.

atmosphere The starfield's usual cool wash gives way to bronze pooling low-right, toward the invitation — the same warmth that lights the moon's near face reappears at the bottom of the screen, so the eye already associates that glow with "the app speaking."

eye-lead A single bronze spine runs from the moon down through lead+dev — text hugs it, indented, until the payoff breaks free (unindented, starlight-colored, its own small bronze glow) — a categorical position + color shift, not a weight step.

invitation No box. A lit phrase in the display face with its own glow and one breathing ember — signals "alive, tap here" the way a lamp signals "on," not the way a button signals "click." Verified against this same render: no background fill, no border, no rounded rect anywhere in .invite — glow + text + ember only.

Fixed bug: the payoff previously broke free via a negative margin that pulled it PAST the spine's x-position, so the spine visually cut through the text. The spine is now geometrically bounded to the lead+dev block alone (a separate wrapper); the payoff sits entirely below it in normal flow — there's no coordinate range left where they can collide, regardless of reading length.

Flagged, not assumed fixed — moon under-lit: two causes found directly in the CSS (see the code comment above .moon-object): the lit-face gradient's bright stops cut off earlier than a real crescent needs, and the inset dark shadow glazed a large share of the sphere regardless of the gradient beneath. Loosened both here, but this is a static browser render — Reanimated's glow pulse and the real feTurbulence grain compositing on an actual phone display read differently. Re-verify brightness on device at the wiring stage, don't treat this render as settled.
