## 1. Register: Formal vs. Informal

### Formal register (официален стил)
- Use **Вие/Вас/Ви** (capitalized) for addressees
- Full sentences, no ellipsis, no slang
- Avoid particles like "бе", "ма", "де", "я"
- Use full article forms correctly (this is scrutinized in formal text)
- Contexts: business correspondence, legal texts, academic writing, official documents, journalism

### Informal register (неофициален стил)
- Use **ти** for addressees
- Shorter sentences, dropped subjects, colloquial vocabulary
- Particles freely used: нали, де, бе, ма, я
- Clitic doubling is very natural: "На мен ми харесва"
- Contexts: conversation, texting, social media, personal emails, creative writing

### The in-between
Most modern Bulgarian writing — blogs, newsletters, casual business emails — lives in a middle register that uses Вие but keeps a conversational tone. This is the safest default for general-purpose text.

## 2. Natural Expression Patterns

### Subject pronoun dropping
Bulgarian drops subject pronouns routinely when the verb conjugation makes the subject obvious. Including "аз" (I) in every sentence sounds robotic and unnatural:
- ✅ "Работя като инженер." (I work as an engineer.)
- ❌ "Аз работя като инженер." (unless emphasizing "I" specifically)

### Clitic doubling is NORMAL
In spoken Bulgarian and natural informal writing, it is standard to double object pronouns:
- "Книгата я прочетох." (The book, I read it.) — perfectly natural
- "На Иван му казах." (To Ivan, I told him.) — standard

This is NOT redundancy — it is a core feature of natural Bulgarian.

### "Да"-constructions replace infinitives everywhere
- "Обичам да чета." (I love to read.)
- "Помогни ми да го направя." (Help me do it.)
- "Няма какво да се прави." (There's nothing to be done.)

### Impersonal constructions
Bulgarian uses many impersonal constructions:
- "Трябва да..." (One must / It is necessary to...)
- "Може да..." (One can / It is possible to...)
- "Не бива да..." (One should not...)
- "Струва ми се, че..." (It seems to me that...)

### The verb "имам" for existential statements
- "Има проблем." (There is a problem.) — NOT *"Един проблем съществува."
- "Няма време." (There's no time.)

## 3. Common Calques and Unnatural Constructions to Avoid

When writing Bulgarian (especially when translating from English), avoid these:

### Passive voice overuse
English uses passive voice frequently. Bulgarian strongly prefers active voice or reflexive constructions:
- ❌ "Писмото беше изпратено от секретарката." (calque)
- ✅ "Секретарката изпрати писмото." (active — far more natural)
- ✅ "Писмото се изпрати." (reflexive passive — natural)

### Literal preposition translation
English prepositions don't map 1:1 to Bulgarian:
- "depend on" → зависи **от** (not *"на")
- "interested in" → интересувам се **от** (not *"в")
- "thank you for" → благодаря **за** (not *"от")
- "consist of" → състои се **от**

### Gerund constructions
English gerunds (-ing forms used as nouns) have no direct equivalent. Use "да" + verb or a verbal noun:
- "I enjoy reading" → "Обичам да чета" (NOT *"Обичам четенето" unless specifically referring to the act of reading as a concept)

### Word-for-word translation of phrasal verbs
English phrasal verbs (look up, find out, give up) should be rendered as single Bulgarian verbs with appropriate aspect:
- "give up" → откажа се / отказвам се
- "find out" → разбера / разбирам
- "look for" → търся

### Overusing "е" (is) in copula constructions
English uses "is" far more than Bulgarian needs "е":
- "The problem is that..." → "Проблемът е, че..." (OK but can be shortened)
- More natural: "Работата е там, че..." (The thing is that...)

## 4. Colloquial and Internet Bulgarian

### Shopi dialect influence (Sofia region)
- "Ко прайш?" (Какво правиш? — What are you doing?)
- Commonly seen online but NOT standard

### Common internet abbreviations and slang
- "бтв" (by the way — borrowed)
- "имо" (in my opinion — borrowed)
- "трябва" sometimes written "тряа" in very casual chat
- Emoji and Latin-script Bulgarian ("shlibaniq" style) are declining but still exist

### Transliteration (шльокавица)
Some Bulgarians type in Latin script when Cyrillic keyboards aren't available. This uses informal phonetic mapping: "Zdravei, kak si?" This is understood but not formal. Cyrillic is always preferred.

### English borrowings
Modern Bulgarian absorbs English words, especially in tech, business, and pop culture: мийтинг, уебсайт, имейл, офис, маркетинг. These are written in Cyrillic and follow Bulgarian grammar (take articles, pluralize, etc.): имейлът, уебсайтовете. However, some brand names are kept in Latin: YouTube, Facebook, iPhone (though Cyrillic variants exist: Ютюб, Фейсбук).

## 5. Idiomatic Expressions and Discourse Markers

Natural Bulgarian text uses discourse markers that have no direct English equivalent:

- **Значи** — "so" / "I mean" (very frequent filler in speech; use sparingly in writing)
- **Де** — softening particle: "Кажи де!" (Come on, tell me!)
- **Май** — "apparently" / "it seems": "Май ще вали." (Looks like it'll rain.)
- **Ами** — "well" (hesitation/transition): "Ами... не знам." (Well... I don't know.)
- **Нали** — "right?" / "isn't it?": "Хубаво е, нали?" (It's nice, right?)
- **Ей** — draws attention: "Ей там!" (Right there!) "Ей, ти!" (Hey, you!)
- **Бе** — emphatic/vocative: "Какво правиш бе?" (What are you doing, man?)
- **Хайде** — "come on" / "let's go": "Хайде, тръгваме!" (Let's go!)

### Common sayings and fixed expressions
- "Няма лошо." — No worries. / That's fine.
- "Да, де." — Yeah, sure. (can be sarcastic)
- "Какво да се прави." — What can you do. (resignation)
- "Не е за вярване." — Hard to believe.
- "И аз така мисля." — I think so too.
- "Тъй де!" — Exactly! / That's what I said!
- "Остави" / "Я остави" — Forget about it. / Drop it.

## 6. Tone and Politeness Conventions

### Greetings
- Formal: "Здравейте!" (Hello, plural/formal)
- Informal: "Здравей!" (Hello, singular/informal), "Здрасти!" (Hi!)
- Very casual: "Ей, здравей!" "Ко стаа?" (What's up? — very colloquial)

### Email conventions
Formal emails typically begin with:
- "Уважаеми г-н/г-жа [Фамилия]," or "Уважаема госпожо [Фамилия],"
- Or simply: "Здравейте,"

And close with:
- "С уважение," (Respectfully,)
- "Поздрави," (Regards,) — semi-formal
- "Благодаря предварително." (Thank you in advance.)

### Diminutives and affection
Bulgarian uses diminutives extensively — they don't just mean "small" but express affection:
- водичка (a nice drink of water), кафенце (a nice little coffee), слънчице (little sun — term of endearment)
- Names: Мария → Маруся, Иван → Ванко/Ванчо, Георги → Гошо

## 7. Writing for Specific Contexts

### Journalism / news
- Formal register, active voice preferred
- Attribution: "каза X" (said X), "според X" (according to X)
- Headlines often drop articles and use present tense for past events

### Academic writing
- Strict formal register, Вие, full sentences
- Passive and impersonal constructions more accepted
- Terms in Cyrillic wherever a Bulgarian equivalent exists

### UI/UX strings
- Usually informal singular (ти): "Запази", "Изпрати", "Избери"
- Or impersonal: "Запазване", "Изпращане"
- Imperative 2nd person singular is the standard for buttons: "Влез" (Log in), "Регистрирай се" (Sign up)

### Creative writing
- Full freedom of register, dialect, archaisms
- The renarrative mood is essential for fairy tales and legends
- The vocative is common in dialogue

### Subtitles
- Conversational register, reflecting the speaker's tone
- Numbers 1–10 written out, above 10 in digits
- Bulgarian quotation marks: „..." or "..."

## 8. Turkish and Other Loanwords

Bulgarian absorbed many Turkish words during Ottoman rule (14th–19th centuries). Many are fully naturalized and common in everyday speech:

- чаршаф (bedsheet), чорап (sock), дюшек (mattress), тефтер (notebook)
- боза (a drink), баница (pastry), кебап (kebab)
- хубав (nice/pretty — from Turkish "hoş")

These are NOT slang — they are standard Bulgarian words. Some have formal "Slavic" alternatives used in higher-register text, but using them in conversation is natural.

Russian, French, Italian, German, and increasingly English loanwords are also common across different domains. Russian influence was especially strong in the 19th–20th centuries for administrative, military, and scientific vocabulary.

## 9. AI-Specific Pitfalls: How Not to Sound Like a Machine

When generating Bulgarian text, watch for these telltale signs of artificial output:

1. **Over-including subject pronouns**: Drop "аз", "ти", "той" when the verb makes it clear. Every sentence starting with "Аз" sounds mechanical.

2. **Stiff word order**: Don't always use SVO. Move elements around for natural flow. Use topicalization and clitic doubling.

3. **Avoiding particles**: Real Bulgarians use нали, ами, значи, де, хайде constantly in informal contexts. Omitting all particles makes text feel sterilized.

4. **Wrong register mixing**: Don't use "бе" in a formal email or "Уважаеми" in a chat message. Match the register to the context.

5. **Over-formal vocabulary**: Using книжовен (literary/bookish) vocabulary where everyday words exist. "Произвеждам" (produce) when "правя" (make/do) is more natural.

6. **Ignoring the definite article logic**: The article goes on the FIRST element. Check every noun phrase.

7. **Perfect tense vs. aorist confusion**: Aorist is for specific completed past events the speaker witnessed. Perfect is for general or non-witnessed past. Using perfect ("четох" vs. "чел съм") where aorist is expected makes the text sound foreign.

8. **Not using the renarrative when retelling**: If describing historical events or hearsay, use the renarrative mood. Using aorist for events the writer couldn't have witnessed is a glaring error.

9. **Translated idioms**: "Имам предвид" is correct for "I mean." But *"Какъв е сделката?" for "What's the deal?" is a horrible calque. Find the Bulgarian equivalent.

10. **Generic Slavic forms**: Don't accidentally use Russian/Serbian forms. Bulgarian has no cases, no infinitive, and a suffixed article — these are its defining features.
