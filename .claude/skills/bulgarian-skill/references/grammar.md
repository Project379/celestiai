## 1. Nouns: Gender, Number, Count Form

Every Bulgarian noun has one of three genders:

**Masculine**: most nouns ending in a consonant — стол (chair), учител (teacher), ден (day).

**Feminine**: most nouns ending in -а/-я — жена (woman), маса (table), земя (earth). Also abstract nouns ending in -ост/-ест (мъдрост, болест, любов), and some zero-ending nouns (кръв, кост, вечер).

**Neuter**: nouns ending in -е/-о — дете (child), езеро (lake). Also some loanwords ending in -и/-у/-ю (цунами, табу, меню).

### Plural formation
- Masculine: commonly -и (факти), -ове (столове), -а/-я in count form
- Feminine: -и (книги, жени)
- Neuter: -а/-я (езера), -та (деца → децата when definite)

### Count form (бройна форма)
Masculine nouns take a special numerical plural with cardinal numbers: два стола, пет стола (NOT *столове when counting). The current norm: use the count form for non-person masculine nouns (два молива). For persons, the general plural is now preferred (двама ученици rather than двама ученика), though both are encountered.

## 2. The Definite Article

Bulgarian has NO separate word for "the." The article is **suffixed** to the first element of the noun phrase. If the noun has an adjective before it, the article goes on the adjective, NOT the noun.

- стол → столът / стола (the chair, subject / object)
- голям стол → големият стол / големия стол (the big chair, subject / object)
- голяма маса → голямата маса (the big table)

### Full vs. Short Article (applies ONLY to masculine singular)

| Form | Suffix | When to use |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Пълен член (full) | -ът / -ят | The word is the **subject** of the sentence |
| Кратък член (short) | -а / -я | Everything else (object, after prepositions, etc.) |

**The "той/него" test**: Replace the articled word with "той" → full article. Replace with "него" → short article.

Examples:
- Учителят влезе в стаята. (Той влезе → full article)
- Видях учителя в коридора. (Видях него → short article)
- На учителя му харесва. (preposition → always short)

**Special cases for full article:**
- After linking verbs (съм, оказвам се, изглеждам, казвам се, ставам): Той е победителят.
- In standalone titles and headings (without a preposition): Прекрасният нов свят
- Appositives and predicatives to the subject: Петър, победителят, получи наградата.

**Exceptions that always take short article even as subject:**
- Nicknames and epithets: Апостола, Щастливеца, Хисарлъка

### Article on feminine and neuter nouns
- Feminine singular: -та (жената, масата, кръвта)
- Neuter singular: -то (детето, езерото)
- All plurals: -те (столовете, жените, децата)

### Article placement in noun phrases
The article attaches to the **first** attributive element:
- хубавият голям стол (NOT *хубавия големият стол)
- моят приятел (NOT *моя приятелят)

## 3. Vocative Case

Bulgarian retains a vocative form for direct address:
- Masculine: -е (Иване!, господине!), -о (Петьо!, татко!)
- Feminine: -о/-е (мамо!, госпожо!, Марийке!)

The vocative is common in spoken language and informal writing. In formal text, it is optional for personal names (especially female and foreign names where it may sound rude or archaic). For common nouns of address (господине, госпожо, бабо, синко), vocative is standard.

## 4. Adjectives

Adjectives agree with their noun in gender, number, and definiteness. They usually precede the noun.

- Masculine: красив (indef.), красивият/красивия (def. full/short)
- Feminine: красива, красивата
- Neuter: красиво, красивото
- Plural: красиви, красивите

### Comparative and superlative
Formed with prefixes (hyphenated): по-красив (more beautiful), най-красив (most beautiful). These prefixes are ALWAYS written with a hyphen.

## 5. Pronouns

### Personal pronouns — subject forms are routinely dropped
Аз, ти, той/тя/то, ние, вие, те — used only for emphasis or contrast. Omit when the verb conjugation makes the subject clear.

### Short (clitic) vs. long pronoun forms
Bulgarian has both full and clitic (short) object pronouns:
- Full: мен, теб, него, нея, нас, вас, тях (used after prepositions or for emphasis)
- Short dative: ми, ти, му, ѝ, ни, ви, им
- Short accusative: ме, те, го, я, ни, ви, ги

**Clitic doubling**: In spoken and informal Bulgarian, it is very common (and grammatically natural) to use both the clitic and the full pronoun: "На мен ми харесва" (I like it). This is NOT an error — it is standard Bulgarian.

**The word "ѝ"**: The short dative feminine pronoun is written with a grave accent: ѝ (to her), to distinguish it from the conjunction и (and). This is one of the most commonly missed diacritics.

### Formal "Вие"
The formal address uses Вие/Вас/Ви/Ваш with capital letters (even mid-sentence) for politeness. This applies in formal correspondence, business writing, and when addressing strangers or superiors.

## 6. Verbs

### No infinitive
Bulgarian has no infinitive. Dictionary entries list the 1st person singular present: пиша (I write), чета (I read), ходя (I walk/go).

Where other languages use infinitive, Bulgarian uses "да + present subjunctive":
- Искам да пиша (I want to write)
- Мога да чета (I can read)
- Трябва да ходя (I must go)

### Conjugation groups
Three conjugation groups based on the vowel of the 3rd person singular present:
- First (е-conjugation): чета → чете
- Second (и-conjugation): ходя → ходи
- Third (а-conjugation): знам → знае / играя → играе

### Aspect
Every Bulgarian verb exists in perfective/imperfective pairs (and sometimes triple):
- пиша (imperfective) / напиша (perfective) — to write
- чета (impf.) / прочета (perf.) — to read

The perfective aspect cannot form a present indicative tense (only present subjunctive with "да").

### Tense system (indicative mood)
- Present (сегашно): чета
- Aorist (минало свършено): четох — completed past action, witnessed
- Imperfect (минало несвършено): четях — ongoing/repeated past action, witnessed
- Perfect (минало неопределено): чел съм — past action with present relevance OR non-witnessed
- Pluperfect (минало предварително): бях чел — had read
- Future (бъдеще): ще чета — will read
- Future in the past (бъдеще в миналото): щях да чета — was going to read
- Future perfect (бъдеще предварително): ще съм чел
- Future perfect in the past (бъд. предв. в мин.): щях да съм чел

### Key verb "ще" for future tense
The future is formed with the invariable particle ще + present tense: ще пиша, ще четеш, ще ходи. Negative future uses "няма да": Няма да пиша (I won't write).

### The "-м" vs. "-ме" ending
First person plural verbs end in -м (пишем, ходим, четем) in present tense but in -ме in imperative (пишете! четете!). This is a common error: "ние пишеме" is WRONG; the correct form is "ние пишем."

## 7. The Renarrative Mood (Преизказно наклонение)

This is unique to Bulgarian among European languages. It marks that the speaker did not personally witness the events they describe — they are retelling what they heard, read, or inferred.

### Formation
The renarrative uses the -л participle forms. In the 3rd person, the auxiliary verb "е"/"са" is **dropped** (this is the key distinguishing feature from the perfect tense):

- Perfect (witnessed/experienced): Той е дошъл. (He has come — I know this.)
- Renarrative (non-witnessed): Той дошъл. (He came, they say / apparently.)

### When to use it
- Retelling historical events the writer didn't witness: "През 681 г. ханът основал държавата." (In 681, the khan founded the state.)
- Reporting what someone else said (indirect speech in past narration)
- Fairy tales and folklore: "Имало едно време един цар..." (Once upon a time there was a king...)
- Expressing doubt or irony: "Той бил много умен!" (He's supposedly very smart!)

### When NOT to use it
- When you are the direct witness of the event (use aorist/imperfect instead)
- In present-tense reporting of current events (news headlines use indicative)
- The renarrative has 5 tenses that parallel the indicative system

## 8. Moods Overview

1. **Indicative (изявително)** — states facts: Пиша. Четох. Ще пиша.
2. **Imperative (повелително)** — commands: Пиши! Четете!
3. **Subjunctive (подчинително)** — wishes, purposes: Да пиша. Да бях писал.
4. **Conditional (условно)** — hypotheticals: Бих писал (I would write). Uses "бих/би/бихме/бихте/биха" + past participle.
5. **Renarrative (преизказно)** — non-witnessed events (see section 7).

## 9. Particles and Clitics

Bulgarian is rich in modal particles:

- **нали** — universal tag question ("isn't it?", "right?"): Ще дойдеш, нали?
- **ли** — question particle, placed after the questioned element: Ти ли си? Идваш ли?
- **нека** — let (hortative): Нека да отидем.
- **дано** — hopefully: Дано дойде.
- **я** — emphatic, mildly imperative: Я виж! (Look here!)
- **хайде/айде** — come on: Хайде, побързай!
- **де** — softening/urging particle: Кажи де!
- **бе** — very informal, emphatic address particle: Какво правиш, бе?
- **ма/бе/ей** — informal vocative particles, strongly colloquial

### Clitic ordering
Bulgarian has strict rules for clitic pronoun placement. Short pronouns cluster before the verb (or after in certain constructions). The ordering within the cluster: dative before accusative (Ще ти го дам — I'll give it to you).

## 10. Negation

Formed with **не** before the verb: Не пиша. Не съм писал.

**Double/triple negation is standard and required:**
- Никой никога нищо не казва. (Nobody ever says anything.) — all negative elements must be present.
- Няма нищо. (There is nothing.)
- Никъде не ходя. (I don't go anywhere.)

Omitting any of the negative words makes the sentence grammatically wrong or changes its meaning.

---

## 11. Word Order

Default: SVO — Аз чета книга. (I read a book.)

But word order is flexible because verb conjugation identifies the subject:
- Книга чета. (emphasizes "a book")
- Чета книга. (neutral, subject dropped)
- Книгата я четох. (clitic doubling with topicalized object — very natural in speech)

**Key rule**: The position of clitics is relatively fixed. They cannot begin a sentence (except in some colloquial speech). The verb or another stressed word must come first: "Чух го" (I heard him), NOT *"Го чух" (in standard language).

## 12. Common Grammar Mistakes

1. **Wrong article**: столА вместо столЪТ (or vice versa) — use the той/него test
2. **Omitting double negation**: *"Никой каза" instead of "Никой не каза"
3. **Using -ме instead of -м** in present tense: *"ние пишеме" → ние пишем
4. **Wrong count form**: *"пет столове" → пет стола
5. **Article on the wrong element**: *"хубавия големият стол" → хубавият голям стол
6. **Missing "ѝ"**: writing "и" instead of "ѝ" for the dative "to her"
7. **Calquing English passive**: Bulgarian prefers active voice far more than English
8. **Forgetting "да"**: *"Искам пиша" → Искам да пиша
9. **Wrong aspect in subjunctive**: "Да пишеш!" (imperfective — do keep writing) vs. "Да напишеш!" (perfective — write it once / get it done)
10. **Preposition confusion**: "в"→"във" before words starting with в/ф; "с"→"със" before words starting with с/з
